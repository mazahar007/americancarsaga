Chevrolet built the first Camaro to answer Ford's Mustang, and by 1969 it had found its look: a lower, wider stance, deeply sculpted sides and that aggressive grille. For many collectors this is *the* Camaro.

## Behind the wheel

Our test car had the 350 small-block and an automatic. It is a relaxed, friendly cruiser that pulls cleanly to highway speed and sounds wonderful doing it. The steering is light with power assist, and with front disc brakes it stops with confidence.

## The SS badge

An SS package brought a stronger V8, special hood, stripes and badges. Because those parts are easy to add, **many ordinary Camaros now wear SS badges**. Check the cowl tag, VIN and any documentation before you pay SS money.

## Ownership costs

Small-block parts are inexpensive and available everywhere. Expect classic-car insurance to be very reasonable on an agreed-value policy, especially if the car is garaged and driven occasionally.

## Should you buy one?

If you want a classic that looks great, drives well and is easy to maintain, a 1969 Camaro is hard to beat. Buy the best body you can find — and enjoy one of Detroit's finest shapes.
