Whether you are buying, selling or insuring a classic, the same question always comes up: *what is it really worth?* Here is a simple method anyone can follow.

## Step 1: Be honest about condition

Most valuation guides use a four-level condition scale:

| Condition | What it means |
|---|---|
| #1 Concours | Among the best in the world. Show-winning, perfect. |
| #2 Excellent | Could win a local show. Very minor flaws. |
| #3 Good | A nice "driver." Looks good from a few feet, some wear. |
| #4 Fair | Runs and drives, but needs work. |

Most cars for sale are honestly a **#3**. Sellers often describe them as #2.

## Step 2: Use valuation tools

Online valuation tools from classic-car insurers and price-tracking sites list typical values for each condition. Look up the exact year, model, body style and engine — these change the price a lot.

## Step 3: Look at real sales, not asking prices

Asking prices are wishes. *Sold* prices are facts. Check recent auction results and completed online sales for cars like yours.

## Step 4: Adjust for what makes your car special

- Matching-numbers engine and transmission
- Original documentation (build sheet, window sticker)
- Rare options or colors
- Known ownership history

## Step 5: Get a professional appraisal when it matters

For insurance, estate planning or a big purchase, a written appraisal from a qualified classic car appraiser is worth the fee.

> A fair value is a range, not a single number. Know the range before you start talking money.
