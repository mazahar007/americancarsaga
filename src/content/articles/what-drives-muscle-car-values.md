Walk through any classic car auction and you will see it: two cars that look almost identical, yet one sells for twice the price of the other. Here is why.

## 1. Originality and documentation

Matching-numbers drivetrains, factory paperwork and correct original parts are the single biggest factor in top-tier prices.

## 2. Rarity of the configuration

The same model can be common or extremely rare depending on engine, transmission and body style. A big-block convertible with a four-speed is a very different car — in value — from a base six-cylinder coupe.

## 3. Condition and quality of restoration

A correct, well-documented restoration by a known specialist commands a premium. A quick cosmetic job usually does not.

## 4. Generational demand

Buyers often want the cars they admired when they were young. As each generation of collectors changes, demand shifts — which is why cars from the 1980s and 1990s have grown in interest in recent years.

## 5. The wider economy

Interest rates and the stock market affect how much money is available for collector cars. Values can flatten or dip when the economy slows.

## 6. Media and pop culture

A starring role in a film or a famous auction sale can lift interest in a model almost overnight.

## The bottom line

Buy a classic because you love it. If it also rises in value, consider that a bonus — not a guarantee.
