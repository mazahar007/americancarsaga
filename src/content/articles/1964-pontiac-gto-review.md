In 1964, a small group of Pontiac engineers did something their bosses had told them not to do. They dropped a big 389 cubic-inch V8 into the mid-size Tempest and called the option package **GTO**. The result changed the American car business forever.

## How it drives today

Turn the key and the 389 settles into a deep, loping idle. Pull away and the first thing you notice is torque — there is plenty of it, right off idle. This is a car that loves to cruise on a two-lane highway at 55 mph with the windows down.

What it does *not* love is hard braking. Standard four-wheel drums need planning ahead, and the manual steering is heavy at parking speeds. Many owners sensibly add front disc brakes and power steering, which make the car far easier to live with.

## What to look for when buying

> The most important rule with any GTO: buy the car with the paperwork, not the story.

- **Check the VIN and documentation.** Because GTO values are high, many Tempests and LeMans have been converted into "tributes." Pontiac Historic Services documentation is worth paying for.
- **Inspect for rust.** Floors, trunk pan, rear quarters and the lower front fenders are the usual suspects.
- **Look at the engine codes.** A matching-numbers engine adds significant value.

## Is it worth it?

If you want a single car that tells the whole story of the muscle-car era, the 1964 GTO is it. It is not the fastest car of the period, but it was the first — and it still feels special every time you drive it.
