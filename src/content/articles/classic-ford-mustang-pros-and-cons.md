Few cars are as easy to love — or as easy to buy badly — as an early Ford Mustang. Ford built well over a million of them in the first two years, so there is a lot of choice. That is good news and bad news.

## The good news

Because so many were built, the parts business behind the Mustang is enormous. Body panels, interiors, trim and engine parts are all reproduced. A first-time classic owner can keep one running with basic tools and a good manual.

## The bad news

That same popularity means many cars have lived hard lives and been repaired many times. A shiny paint job can hide a lot.

**Check the cowl** (the area under the base of the windshield). It is a well-known rust trap that lets water into the footwells. Repairs are possible but not cheap.

## Our advice

1. Decide what you want first: a weekend cruiser, a show car or an investment.
2. Pay for a professional pre-purchase inspection.
3. Buy the best body you can afford — mechanical work is cheaper than rust repair.

An early Mustang is still one of the best entry points into classic car ownership. Go in with your eyes open, and it will reward you for years.
