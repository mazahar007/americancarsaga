A few hours of preparation in the fall can save you hundreds of dollars — and a lot of frustration — in the spring. Here is our simple checklist.

## 1. Wash, dry and wax

Road grime and bird droppings can eat into paint over months. Clean the car thoroughly, including underneath, and let it dry completely.

## 2. Fill the tank and add stabilizer

A full tank leaves less room for moisture. Add a fuel stabilizer and run the engine for a few minutes so it reaches the carburetor or injectors.

## 3. Change the oil

Used oil contains acids that can harm engine parts during storage. Fresh oil is cheap insurance.

## 4. Look after the battery

Connect a quality **battery maintainer** (a "smart" trickle charger), or remove the battery and store it somewhere cool and dry.

## 5. Protect the tires

Inflate tires to the maximum pressure shown on the sidewall, or place the car on jack stands to avoid flat spots.

## 6. Keep pests out

Place steel wool in the exhaust tip (with a reminder note on the steering wheel!) and use rodent deterrents around the car.

## 7. Cover it

Use a soft, **breathable** car cover. See our [car cover buying guide](/articles/best-car-covers-for-classic-cars).

## In the spring

Remove the steel wool, check fluids and tire pressures, reconnect the battery, and enjoy that first drive of the season.
