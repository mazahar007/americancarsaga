Opened in 1926, U.S. Route 66 ran roughly 2,400 miles from Chicago to Santa Monica. It became the most famous road in America — and a big part of the reason Americans fell in love with their cars.

## A road of hope

In the 1930s, families escaping the Dust Bowl loaded everything they owned into old sedans and trucks and headed west on 66. John Steinbeck called it "the mother road."

## The golden age of the road trip

After World War II, new cars, paid vacations and growing families turned Route 66 into a holiday route. Motels with neon signs, diners, gas stations and roadside attractions sprang up along the way. The trip itself became the adventure.

## Bypassed, but not forgotten

The Interstate Highway System gradually replaced Route 66, and it was officially removed from the U.S. highway system in 1985. Yet long stretches survive, many marked as "Historic Route 66," and travelers from around the world come to drive them.

> There is no better place for a classic American car than the road it was built for.

## Planning your own drive

Spring and autumn offer the best weather. Take your time, stop often, and talk to the people who keep the road's small towns alive.
