If your classic car is insured on a regular auto policy, you may be in for a shock after an accident. Standard policies usually pay *actual cash value* — what the insurer decides an old car is worth, minus depreciation. For a classic, that can be far less than its true market value.

## Agreed value

You and the insurer **agree on the car's value when the policy starts**. If the car is a total loss, you receive that amount (minus any deductible). This is what most classic owners want.

## Stated value

You *state* a value, but the insurer may pay **the lesser of** the stated value or actual cash value. It sounds similar, but it is not the same protection.

## Questions to ask any insurer

1. Is this an *agreed value* policy?
2. What are the mileage limits?
3. What are the storage requirements?
4. Is there coverage for spare parts and tools?
5. Can I increase the value as my car appreciates or after restoration?

## Our advice

Get at least two quotes from specialist classic car insurers, and keep photos and receipts of all work done on your car. Good records make agreeing on a value much easier.
